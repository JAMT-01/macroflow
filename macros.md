## 2. Where the Calorie Number Comes From

**BMR (Mifflin–St Jeor)** = `10×96 + 6.25×193 − 5×25 + 5` = **2,046 kcal**

| Activity level | Multiplier | Maintenance (TDEE) | Bulk target (+300–400) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desk job, no training | 1.20 | 2,456 | 2,800 |
| Desk job + lifting 3×/wk | 1.375 | 2,814 | **3,150** |
| Lifting 4–5×/wk + 8–10k steps | 1.55 | 3,172 | **3,500** |
| Lifting 6×/wk + physical job / 12k+ steps | 1.725 | 3,530 | 3,850 |

**Default assumption used in this doc:** you train 3–4×/week with an otherwise sedentary day → maintenance ≈ **2,800 kcal**, target ≈ **3,110 kcal**. This matches `activity: light` in your app settings, and the app's 3,110 kcal target lands almost exactly on the 1.375 row — that number is sound; leave it until scale data says otherwise.

If your daily steps are genuinely high (10k+), move to the 1.55 row.

### Surplus size

Bigger surplus ≠ more muscle. Muscle protein synthesis has a ceiling; surplus calories beyond it become fat. Evidence-based range for a beginner:

- **+250–400 kcal/day** — the sweet spot. Beginners tolerate the upper end because "newbie gains" are real and muscle-to-fat partitioning is temporarily favorable.
- **+500 kcal/day and up** — you gain faster, but the extra is disproportionately fat. Not worth it.

### Target rate of gain

| Rate | Weekly | Monthly | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25% BW | 0.24 kg | 1.0 kg | Conservative, very lean |
| **0.35% BW** | **0.34 kg** | **1.5 kg** | **Recommended** |
| 0.50% BW | 0.48 kg | 2.1 kg | Upper limit — beginners only |
| > 0.75% BW | > 0.7 kg | > 3 kg | Mostly fat. Cut calories. |

Aim for **+0.25 to +0.4 kg per week measured as a 7-day rolling average.** Anything faster than 0.5 kg/wk sustained means the surplus is too big.

## 4. Protein — The One You Must Not Miss

### The number

Morton et al. (2018), the largest meta-analysis on the topic (49 studies, 1,863 subjects), found a **breakpoint at ~1.62 g/kg/day** — beyond that, extra protein stopped adding fat-free mass. But the 95% confidence interval ran to **2.2 g/kg**, which is why that figure is the standard practical ceiling. The ISSN position stand recommends **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day**, with up to 2.2 g/kg for maximizing lean mass.

| Intake | Grams/day for you | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1.6 g/kg | 155 g | Statistical minimum for full effect |
| **2.0 g/kg** | **192 g** | **Your app's target — buffer above the breakpoint** |
| 2.2 g/kg | 211 g | Upper bound; no proven extra benefit |

**Why aim above 1.62 rather than at it:** you are tracking with a photo app that has 25–35% error. A target of 192 g that lands at 155 g on a bad day still clears the threshold. A target of 155 g that lands at 125 g does not. **The buffer is insurance against your own measurement error.**

### Distribution

Schoenfeld & Aragon (2018): consume **≥ 0.4 g/kg per meal across ≥ 4 meals**. For you:

- **0.4 g/kg = 38 g minimum per meal**
- 4 meals × 48 g = 192 g/day ✅
- Each meal should carry **~2.5–3 g leucine** — hit automatically by 40 g+ of animal protein, whey, or a soy/pea blend.
- Space meals **3–4 hours** apart.

### Practical protein anchors (approximate, per 100 g cooked)

| Food | Protein | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast | 31 g | Cheapest lean staple |
| Lean beef 95% | 28 g | Adds iron, zinc, creatine |
| Whey isolate (per 30 g scoop) | 25 g | Easiest way to close a gap |
| Greek yogurt 0% | 10 g | Good carrier for fruit + fiber |
| Eggs (per egg) | 6 g | Fat comes with it |
| Canned tuna | 26 g | Cap at ~3×/wk for mercury |
| Cottage cheese | 11 g | Slow-digesting, good pre-sleep |
| Lentils (cooked) | 9 g | Doubles as a fiber source |
| Firm tofu | 17 g | Complete plant protein |

**Rule of thumb:** a palm-sized portion of meat or fish ≈ 30–40 g protein. Learn this and you can eyeball protein without the app.

## 5. Fat

| Threshold | g/day | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Physiological floor | 48 g (0.5 g/kg) | Below this: measurable hormonal, immune, neurological cost |
| Sports-nutrition minimum | ~70 g (20% kcal) | Standard lower bound for athletes |
| **Target** | **85–95 g (0.9–1.0 g/kg)** | **Recommended** |
| Upper practical | 120 g (~33% kcal) | Fine, but squeezes carbs |

Intake below 15–20% of calories is associated with reduced testosterone in male athletes. You have no reason to go near that on a bulk — fat is calorie-dense and helps you reach 3,110 kcal without feeling stuffed.

**Composition matters more than the number:**

- Prioritize mono- and polyunsaturated: olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish.
- **Omega-3 (EPA+DHA): target 1–2 g/day** — 2–3 servings of fatty fish per week, or a supplement. This is the fat sub-target most worth tracking.
- Keep saturated fat under ~10% of calories (~35 g). Not a hypertrophy issue; a cardiovascular one.
- Trans fat: zero.

## 6. Carbs

Not "optional." Carbohydrate maintains muscle glycogen, which drives training volume, which drives hypertrophy. The evidence-based range for resistance training is **4–7 g/kg/day** for high-volume work and **3–5 g/kg** for general fitness. Your 392 g at 3,110 kcal = **4.1 g/kg** — right in the pocket.

- **Around training:** 40–80 g carbs in the 1–3 h before lifting improves session quality. Post-workout timing is far less critical than the daily total — the "anabolic window" is hours wide, not minutes.
- **Source split:** aim for ~80% from whole or minimally processed sources (rice, oats, potato, fruit, legumes, whole grains). The remaining 20% as whatever gets you to your calorie target is fine and helps adherence.
- **Carbs are the release valve.** Too full to eat? Swap some carbs for fat (denser). Gaining too fast? Cut carbs first — never protein.

## 7. Fiber — Dedicated Section

You asked about this specifically, and it's the one most people on a bulk get wrong in *both* directions.

### The target

The accepted adequate intake is **14 g per 1,000 kcal**, which is where the standard "38 g for adult men" figure comes from. At your 3,110 kcal target that scales to **43 g** — which is now what the app is set to.

| Your calorie level | Fiber by 14 g/1000 kcal |
|---|---|
| 2,900 | 41 g |
| **3,110** | **43 g** |
| 3,300 | 46 g |
| 3,500 | 49 g |

**Practical target: 43 g/day. Floor: 30 g.** Don't chase 50 g+ — no added benefit, and it actively works against you (see below).

> **This was the single biggest gap between the research and your app.** MacroFlow was set to a 30 g fiber target — a generic default, below even the 38 g baseline for adult men, and 13 g under what your calorie intake calls for. It has been corrected to 43 g. If your logged average has been landing near 30 g and reading as "on target," it was actually about 70% of where it should be.

### The bulk-specific problem

Fiber increases satiety and slows gastric emptying. On a **surplus** that fights you: you need to eat 3,110 kcal and fiber is making you feel full at 2,600. Meanwhile a bulk mechanically pushes fiber up because you're eating more total food.

**How to resolve it:**

- Get fiber from **calorie-dense** sources, not calorie-sparse ones. Oats, beans, lentils, whole grains, dried fruit, nuts, avocado all deliver fiber *with* calories. Raw salad greens and celery deliver fiber with almost none, and fill you up for nothing.
- Push the bulkiest fiber to meals **away from training** — you don't want 15 g of insoluble fiber sitting in your gut during squats.
- If you're consistently missing calories, drop fiber toward the 30–35 g floor rather than missing the surplus.

### Ramping up safely

If you're currently eating ~15–20 g/day (typical), do **not** jump to 45 g. That produces gas, bloating, and cramping.

- Increase by **3–5 g every 3–4 days**.
- Give the gut **2–4 weeks** to adapt at each plateau.
- **Water is mandatory.** Fiber without adequate fluid causes constipation, not relief. Every fiber increase needs a water increase.
- Diarrhea → shift toward **soluble** fiber (oats, bananas, peeled apples, psyllium). Constipation → shift toward **insoluble** (whole grains, vegetable skins, nuts) and add water.
- Highly fermentable fibers (inulin, chicory root, resistant starch, most "fiber-added" protein bars) cause the most gas. Introduce these last and slowest.

### Soluble vs insoluble

| Type | Does | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Soluble** | Gels; lowers LDL, blunts glucose spikes, feeds gut bacteria | Oats, beans, lentils, apples, citrus, psyllium, barley |
| **Insoluble** | Adds bulk; speeds transit, bowel regularity | Whole wheat, bran, nuts, seeds, vegetable skins, brown rice |

Aim for a rough **1:2 to 1:3 soluble:insoluble** ratio — you get this automatically from a varied whole-food diet without tracking it.

### High-value fiber foods

| Food | Serving | Fiber | Calories |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black beans | 1 cup cooked | 15 g | 227 |
| Lentils | 1 cup cooked | 15 g | 230 |
| Chia seeds | 30 g | 10 g | 145 |
| Avocado | 1 medium | 10 g | 240 |
| Rolled oats | 80 g dry | 8 g | 300 |
| Raspberries | 1 cup | 8 g | 64 |
| Whole wheat pasta | 100 g dry | 8 g | 350 |

**A daily template that lands ~42 g:** oats + berries at breakfast (14 g), a cup of beans or lentils with lunch (15 g), whole-grain carb + vegetables at dinner (8 g), nuts or fruit as a snack (5 g).

### Distribution

Spread it — **8–12 g per main meal, 3–5 g in snacks.** Dumping 30 g into one meal is the fastest route to bloating.

## 9. Using a Photo/LLM Macro App — Accuracy & Calibration

The most important section for your setup, because your tool has a known and quantified error profile.

### What the research says

2025 peer-reviewed testing of current AI models on food photos:

- **Food identification:** ~68–93% accuracy depending on complexity
- **Calorie estimation, image only:** **26–36% mean absolute percentage error**
- **Calorie estimation, image + typed ingredient details:** drops to **~14%**
- **Portion-size estimation alone:** as low as 39% accurate — the weakest link
- Simple single foods estimate well; mixed dishes, sauces, and restaurant plates estimate badly

**Translation:** your app reporting "3,110 kcal" means your real intake was somewhere in **2,250–4,000 kcal**. As an absolute measurement, that's close to useless.

### Why it's still fine

**The error is largely systematic, not random.** You eat similar foods, photograph from similar angles, and the model makes similar mistakes each time. So while the absolute number is wrong, the *direction and magnitude of change* is reliable. If the app says you went from 3,110 to 3,400, you really did eat more.

**Don't trust the number — trust the trend, and calibrate the target against the scale.**

### The calibration protocol

1. **Weeks 1–2:** eat normally, log every meal, weigh yourself daily (first thing, after the bathroom, before eating). Compute the 7-day average.
2. **If weight is stable across those two weeks:** whatever the app says you averaged **is your maintenance in app-units** — even if that number reads 2,600 or 3,700. It's wrong in absolute terms and correct in app terms.
3. **Set your target = app-maintenance + 300.** From here on your goal is the app's number, and it works because both sides of the equation use the same biased ruler.
4. **Re-verify every 4–6 weeks**, because TDEE rises as you gain weight.

This is why the 3,110 kcal in Section 1 is a *starting hypothesis*. After two weeks you replace it with a number derived from your own data.

### Getting more out of the app

- **Add a text note with the photo** — "180 g chicken breast, 250 g cooked rice, 1 tbsp olive oil." This alone cuts error from ~30% to ~14%. Highest-leverage habit on this list.
- **Put a size reference in frame** — a fork, your hand, a standard plate. Portion size is the app's weakest dimension.
- **Weigh your protein sources only.** Protein is the macro you can't afford to miss and the easiest to weigh (meat, yogurt, powder come in discrete units). Let the app estimate everything else. 20% of the effort for 80% of the accuracy.
- **Shoot before eating, from a 45° angle**, one photo per component for mixed plates.
- **Log the oil.** Cooking fat is invisible in a photo and is the single largest source of underestimation — a tablespoon is 120 kcal and the app will never see it.
- **Build a library of repeat meals.** Once you've logged a meal carefully, reuse the entry. Five or six rotating meals covering 80% of your week makes tracking nearly free.

| **Key lift loads** | Every session | Rising over 4-week blocks | The real proof muscle is being built |
| **Photos** | Every 4 weeks | Same light, pose, time of day | Catches what the scale can't |
| **Sleep** | Daily | 7–9 h | |
| **Steps** | Daily | Keep *consistent* | Changing NEAT silently changes your TDEE |

**If the waist is growing as fast as bodyweight, the surplus is too large — regardless of what the scale says.**

> ### ⚠️ The one thing currently blocking everything else
>
> As of 2026-08-18 MacroFlow holds **14 logged meals** (most recent: today) and **1 weight entry** — recorded 2026-08-13 and never updated.
>
> Food logging is the habit that's working. Weight logging is the one that isn't, and it's the more important of the two, because **every feedback mechanism in this document runs on the weight trend, not the food log**:
>
> - **Section 9's calibration protocol** — the thing that makes a 30%-error photo app usable at all — needs 14 days of daily weights. It cannot run on one data point.
> - **Section 11's adjustment algorithm** compares this week's 7-day average to last week's. With one entry there is no average and no delta, so there is no signal telling you whether 3,110 kcal is too much, too little, or right.
> - **The weekly/monthly LLM reports** pull `recentWeights` into their payload. Right now the model is being asked to assess a bulk while blind to whether the person is actually gaining.
>
> Without this, you are logging food precisely and flying blind on the only outcome that matters. **Weigh daily, same conditions, and log it.** Thirty seconds a day, and it converts the whole system from descriptive to self-correcting.

## 11. The Adjustment Algorithm

Run this every **2 weeks** using 7-day rolling averages:

```
weekly_gain = (this week's 7-day avg) - (last week's 7-day avg)

if weekly_gain < 0.15 kg for 2 consecutive weeks:
    calories += 200        # add as carbs
elif weekly_gain > 0.50 kg for 2 consecutive weeks:
    calories -= 200        # remove from carbs and fat
else:
    hold
```

**Never adjust on a single week.** Water, sodium, glycogen, and digestive contents swing daily weight by 1–2 kg — larger than the signal you're looking for.

**Protein never changes.** Only carbs and fat move.

## 12. Training Minimums

Macros without a training stimulus produce fat, not muscle. The nutrition above only works on top of:

- **Frequency:** 3–4 sessions/week. Full-body ×3 or upper/lower ×4 — both excellent for a beginner.
- **Volume:** **10–20 direct sets per muscle group per week.** The 2025 dose-response meta-regression found gains keep rising with volume but with clear diminishing returns. Start at the low end (10) and add sets over months — starting high leaves you nowhere to progress.
- **Intensity:** most sets taken to **0–3 reps in reserve**. Rep ranges of 5–30 all build muscle when effort is high; 6–15 is the practical sweet spot.
- **Progressive overload:** add reps or load nearly every session in your first year. This is the actual driver — everything else is support.
- **Movements:** squat/leg press, hinge (RDL/deadlift), horizontal push, vertical push, horizontal pull, vertical pull. Add direct arm, calf, and lateral-delt work.
- **Cardio:** 2 × 20–30 min moderate sessions/week. Doesn't meaningfully interfere at this volume, and it improves work capacity and recovery.

At 193 cm you have long limbs — expect longer ranges of motion and slower initial barbell progress than shorter lifters. That's leverage, not weakness. Don't let it push you into ego loading and bad positions.

## 14. Reality Checks

- **Newbie gains are a limited-time offer.** Your first 6–12 months are the fastest muscle gain you will ever experience. Don't waste them on program-hopping, inconsistency, or a bulk so aggressive you have to cut it all back off.
- **Estimate your body fat first.** At 96 kg / 193 cm, BMI 25.8 says nothing useful on its own. Above ~20% body fat, consider 8–12 weeks at maintenance first (recomp — beginners can build muscle and lose fat simultaneously). At or below ~15–18%, start the surplus now. A cheap caliper, a DEXA scan, or an honest mirror check all work.
- **Weight gain is not linear.** You'll see nothing for 10 days and then 0.8 kg overnight. Only the average matters.
- **Adherence beats optimization.** A plan you hit 6 days a week at 85% precision destroys a perfect plan you hit 3 days a week. The photo app is the right call precisely *because* it's low-friction.
- **Perfect tracking is not the goal.** You're using a tool with 30% error. Chasing gram-precision inside that error band is theater. Hit protein, hit fiber, hit calories within ~10%, get stronger every week, sleep. That's the whole game.
